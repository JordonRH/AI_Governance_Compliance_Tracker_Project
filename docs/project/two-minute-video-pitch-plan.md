# AITrace two-minute video pitch

**Status:** Draft for team review. This plan deliberately does not include external video research; that work will happen after the showcase.

## Aim

Present the Australian SME AI-governance problem, the AITrace prototype, evidence that the implemented workflow is viable, what the team learned, and the next steps. Target **1 minute 50 seconds to 1 minute 55 seconds**, leaving a small buffer under the two-minute limit.

Use four short lower-thirds to identify the speakers. The lower-third contribution descriptions must be agreed by the team and reflect each person’s actual contribution; the proposed speaking order below is only a recording role, not a claim about authorship.

## Recording plan

1. Record each speaker in a quiet, well-lit space at eye level. Use the same framing and audio setup where possible.
2. Keep screen captures to short, legible moments; do not try to demonstrate every feature. The current desktop sidebar, dashboard, register, assessment/action and reporting views are enough.
3. Use only the project’s fictional/de-identified showcase data. Do not present the prototype as a production deployment, sponsor-approved rule set or legal-compliance certification.
4. Add captions and a title/end card: **AITrace — responsible AI governance for Australian SMEs**.
5. Rehearse twice with a timer. Aim for the supplied script to finish between 1:50 and 1:55, then record a clean take with natural pauses.
6. Before export, check that names, contribution labels, sponsor statements and the test counts are current. Export at 1080p with clear speech louder than any background music.

## Four-speaker script

The script is about 225 words at a measured presentation pace. Replace the lower-third contribution labels with team-confirmed facts before recording.

### Speaker 1 — Jordon (0:00–0:27)

“AI is already helping many small businesses write, analyse and respond. But when a tool is adopted informally, people can lose sight of who owns it, what data it handles and what follow-up is needed. That is the governance gap AITrace addresses: making AI use visible before it becomes a blind spot.”

### Speaker 2 — Avnish (0:27–0:55)

“AITrace is a local prototype for Australian SMEs. Staff can disclose an unregistered tool, while managers register approved uses with a purpose, owner, business area and data sensitivity. They can then complete a governance assessment, see an explainable demonstration result, and turn the result into assigned actions, policy evidence and reminders.”

### Speaker 3 — Ashvin (0:55–1:23)

“We built the workflow as a connected record rather than a checklist: register, assess, follow up, retain evidence and report. The prototype supports three roles, organisation-scoped access, versioned policies, a dashboard and PDF or CSV summaries. We tested the implemented workflow with fictional data: 68 application tests and 23 browser tests currently pass.”

### Speaker 4 — Noorpreet (1:23–1:55)

“Our key learning was that useful governance is not only a scoring model. It needs clear language, visible ownership and a process people can actually follow. Next, we will demonstrate AITrace to our sponsor, use their feedback to refine the workflow, and seek approval for the assessment content. AITrace is a practical starting point for clarity, accountability and responsible AI use for SMEs.”
